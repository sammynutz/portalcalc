# Changelog

All notable PortalCalc desktop releases should be recorded here.

## Unreleased

- **One frame optimiser. The legacy local-search optimiser is removed; Optimise is the stiffness-first search (formerly "Physics Optimise") everywhere.**
  - It now runs from the Optimise button (the separate Physics Optimise button is gone), Optimise frame + bracing, the calculation service's `autoSize`, the building-load screening gate, Configurator Review and the hay-shed search. Optimise frame + bracing had been sizing every frame with the legacy search.
  - New **depth sweep** after the local refinement (`auto_size_depth_sweep`). At each truss depth over the permitted range (100 mm steps, then ±50 mm around the cheapest) the sections are sized for that depth's own envelopes, repaired to an exact pass, and the cheapest gets a short cheaper-neighbour descent. The stiffness screen ranks by serviceability and so favoured deep trusses; neither old search could make the coupled depth + section move.
  - Optimise reports progress (steps 1–12) to the Optimise dialog and the Optimise frame + bracing bar.
  - Removed with it: the optimisation learning store and warm starts (File > Import Optimiser Warm Starts..., surrogate predictions), the guided-strategy hooks and 43 legacy-only methods (about 2,600 lines of `main.py`).
  - Results:

    | | Legacy | Optimise before the sweep | Optimise now |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Factory defaults | $35,404 (1000 mm, 250UB31.4) | $37,365 (1300 mm, 360UB44.7) | $35,445 (1100 mm, 310UB32) |
    | PortalTest2 2D | $60,129 | $57,232 | $56,958 (850 mm) |
    | PortalTest2 frame + bracing | $66,983 | – | $62,853.68 |

  - Time: a single Optimise takes about 40–50 s (was 23–36 s); Optimise frame + bracing on PortalTest2 294 s. The calculation service `autoSize` baseline is updated.

- **Changes main portal results. Eave height is now the roof cladding pitching point (outside of the girts); the top chord node sits below it.**
  - Node at the column centrelines = H + (g + D/2)·tan(pitch) − (p + t/2)/cos(pitch). g is the selected girt depth, D the column depth, p the purlin depth (the deeper of the general and edge-zone selections) and t the top chord (or rafter) depth. A girt or purlin with no passing section uses 200 mm, and the results say so.
  - Building width stays to the outside of the columns. A gable uses the mean of both sides' g + D/2 (ridge on the truss centreline); a skillion uses its low side.
  - Purlins and girts are selected before the frame is built, from the building envelope only (the purlin spacing doesn't depend on the node height), so section changes during sizing only move the node by a few mm.
  - The envelope outside the frame lines is carried to the eave nodes in every load case: the wall from the node up to the pitching point, and the roof from the column centreline out to the cladding line. A side whose canopy/lean-to roof starts at the eave node keeps its attachment members' loads instead. Crane and storage loads are not extended.
  - Wind, wall girt spacing, end walls, opening areas and the H/n sway limit still use the entered eave height.
  - Results list the eave height, node height and its working, and the eave strips.
  - Effect: PortalTest2 (6.0 m eave, 250 purlins, 530UB columns, 5°): node 5,740 mm (was 6,000). Factory defaults: auto-size keeps its sections; governing BOTTOM Combined 0.974 → LEFT_COLUMN Combined 0.978. Check-specified 1.758 → 1.776 (calculation service baseline updated). Recheck existing jobs.
  - Known: on PortalTest2 the 2D auto-size now stops at a 650 mm truss with 75×4 chords and 310UB40.4/360UB50.7 columns ($60,129). The earlier design (950 mm, 75×3 chords, 310UB32/360UB44.7) still passes on the new basis (0.991, $57,148): the local search no longer takes the depth step. Optimise frame + bracing: $66,983.33 (was $63,550.75), from the same 2D miss.

- **Bays are entered as a list (Geometry > Main geometry > Bays (mm)), replacing Building Length and Tributary bay size.**
  - Entry: `7*8000, 7000` or `3*8000 2*7500 8000` (count*length in mm, separated by commas or spaces). The summary below shows the length, bay count and design bay; unreadable text keeps the last valid bays and says so.
  - The building length is the sum of the bays. A single-frame design uses the **longest bay** as its tributary width and purlin/girt span (conservative for every frame). Building loads already give each gridline its own tributary.
  - Gridlines follow the actual bays everywhere: the 3D bracing layout, building loads, costing frame count and the concept 3D view (which previously spread frames evenly).
  - Presets save `geometry.bays_mm` and still write `wind.building_length_m` and `loads.bay_size_*` (derived). Older presets convert by the old rule (full bays from the front, then a shorter last bay), so their gridlines and saved bracing locations are unchanged. BOLT `frame_preset` writes the bay list too.
- **3D bracing works with unequal bays.** It had stopped every layout trial on a building whose length was not a whole number of bays ("Installed roof bending basis does not match the actual bay span"), and Optimise frame + bracing reported only "No passing practical layout found". The installed purlins are checked at the design (longest) bay, which now covers any shorter bay. A bay longer than that check still fails.
  - The preliminary 50/50 roof-run check analyses the run with its actual spans and gives every bay the longest bay's table reference. A shorter bay's own reference is higher, so this is conservative.
  - PortalTest2 (21 x 63 m, 7*8000 + 7000, open right wall, 6 m canopy): Optimise frame + bracing now completes at $63,550.75 (full roof bracing in the anchor bays with girders at both eaves; frame sized for 0.109 kN/mm). The same building at 64 m with equal bays: $63,996.42.
  - Results with equal bays are unchanged (smoke suite; calculation service v1 baseline).

- **Building loads: each gridline checked with its own loads (new "Frame loads" selector, Geometry/Wind tab).**
  - **"Single frame (2D envelope)"** is today's behaviour and the default. Presets without the setting load unchanged.
  - **"Building loads (per gridline)"** applies wind and gravity to the building and checks every gridline's own frame:
    - half bays at the end frames;
    - end wind from both ends, each with its own Md;
    - Table 5.3(A) roof and 5.2(C) side-wall bands by position, with d = building length at every pitch;
    - Cpi from where each opening is;
    - Kc by AS/NZS 1170.2 effective surfaces (roof, clad column lines, internal at |Cpi| >= 0.4: 3+ -> 0.8, 2 -> 0.9, 1 -> 1.0).
  - **"Building loads, 2D coefficients"** checks every gridline with today's coefficients, for comparison. One interior gridline reproduces today's envelope exactly.
  - **Optimiser:** sizes on the governing combinations and accepts only on a check of all of them.
  - **Factory defaults:** $34,048.47 against $35,404.48 (-3.8%), 28 s against 20 s. A 6 m lean-to + 4 m canopy building: -3.5%.
  - **Results:** the governing gridline and case are listed per member action.
  - **Pitches of 10 degrees and over:** the end-wind roof uplift near the windward end can be up to 34% above today's single-frame value. Today's 2D used the uniform downwind-rafter Cpe; Table 5.3(A) is more severe within 1h of the end.
  - **Fixed:** the optimiser could capture a stale "protected" design from the previous run after a preset reload. It now re-solves first; today's results are unchanged.
  - **Calculation service v2** (`portalcalc.calculation.request.v2`) takes a 3D building from BOLT (`portalcalc.building.v1`):
    - canopies and lean-tos, full or part length;
    - lightweight propped end frames (pinned props checked for frame axial + end-wall wind; eaves held by end-wall bracing springs);
    - several sizing groups, each sized as its own 2D frame.

    It returns per-gridline sections, governing evidence and a resubmittable sized building. v1 requests and digests are unchanged.

  See `BUILDING_LOADS_3D_PLAN.md` and `CALCULATION_SERVICE.md`. Source only; no release packaged.
- **IL1 column sway relaxed to H/80 (was H/100).** The project default for IL1 in-plane eave sway (`SERVICEABILITY_LIMIT_DEFAULTS['column_il1']`) is now H/80, and the 3D global longitudinal sway follows it; IL2+ stays H/150 and storage buildings keep their H/100 eave limit. Presets that never overrode the limit pick it up. The 2D auto-size of factory defaults is unchanged (sway did not govern), and so is the factory optimiser result ($38,514.03). Optimise frame + bracing: PortalTest1 $151,114.58 (was $151,787.33, still full roof bracing); Bracingtest1 $89,985.86 (was $90,525.29), now the continuous two-strip left-eave girder with the frame sized for 0.555 kN/mm. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Portal frame brace checks refined.** Members are checked segment by segment between lateral restraints, each with its own peak moment and AS 4100 Table 5.6.1 moment modifier from its end moments (columns about 1.1 in the governing top segment, 1.75 over a full unrestrained height; beam 2.5 in double curvature), capped at phi Ms. Columns keep the sway in-plane effective length 2.0 h (pinned-base sway frame, about 1.96 from the AS 4100 sway chart for a typical brace; a braced 0.7 does not apply). New **Portal brace beam LTB kr** (default 0.85; 0.7 with both knees restraining the flanges) and **Portal brace beam restraint** (default 0 = the bay). On PortalTest1 the portal brace members reach 0.39-0.49 utilisation with global sway at 0.95-1.0, so their sizes are set by sway stiffness and these refinements do not change them. Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: portal frame braces.** A side-wall bay that can't take X bracing (open, blocked, a door) can now take a portal frame brace: UB columns stitch-welded inside the two portal columns and a UB beam moment-connected across the top at eave level, on pinned bases. Each is a 2D wall frame condensed onto the two eave Y translations (`bracing_portal_brace.py`; matches an independent `Structure2D` solve and the closed-form sway stiffness within 1%), with its N/V/M and base reactions recovered in every case and included in the force balance and load-path evidence. New design groups **Portal brace columns (UB)** and **Portal brace beam (UB)**: AS 4100-style combined checks per case (columns 2.0 h in-plane, minor/LTB = **Portal brace column restraint**, default 1500 mm; beam full span), UB stock-length cost, and an **Allowance per portal brace** (default $0). They are sized by auto-size and the SLS stiffness screen (by E·I) and count as side-wall bracing for the braced-bay spacing rule. Layout: new **Left/Right wall portal brace** locations in the 3D editor (pin/block under their face filter; pinned ones drawn as a column-beam frame), schema `bracing-layout.v3` only when used; a bay can't have both X bracing and a portal brace. Find layout keeps portal braces you pin; Optimise frame + bracing also tries them on a side wall whose end bays can't take X bracing (end bays, every anchor bay, end bays with an eave girder). On PortalTest1 the best portal option (end bays, 410UB53.7 columns, 530UB82 beam, wire elsewhere) is $25,100 of bracing against $17,746 for full roof bracing, so full roof is kept; on Bracingtest1 the end-bay option sizes to $26,759 against $12,999. Results without portal braces are unchanged (factory defaults $38,514.03). Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: fewer canopy braces and leaner full-roof options.** Canopies are now braced in their end roof-braced bays plus only the bays the braced-bay spacing rule needs (`canopy_bays`), instead of every roof-braced bay: a 7-bay full roof braces the canopy in bays 1, 4 and 7. Optimise frame + bracing also tries full roof bracing in the spacing-rule anchor bays with roof girders at both eaves, and in alternate bays. On PortalTest1 (36 x 63 m, right wall open) only full roof bracing in every bay passes among the roof options; on Bracingtest1 the anchor-bay variant passes at $15,432 against $12,999. Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: stressed-skin credit on open canopy roofs.** With the SLS stressed-skin roof option on, each open canopy now also gets one sheeting panel per bay, from the main eave line to the canopy edge purlin, as crossed tension-only equivalent diagonals on the existing eave and canopy-edge nodes. There is no separate canopy setting: canopies use the roof c and, in Rev B geometry mode, the roof a/b^2 proxy with a = bay length and b = sloped canopy width, so narrow canopies are much softer than the main roof (a 2.5 m canopy in an 8 m bay: 65.7 mm/kN against 45.6 mm/kN for a 3 m canopy). SLS only, as for the roof; ULS, costs and member checks are unchanged. The report adds an "Open canopy roofs" line and a `canopy` panel count, and the workspace summary counts the canopy panels. In the regression fixture (canopies braced in every bay) the sheets reduce canopy-edge movement and edge-to-eave racking; the eave line is held by the roof bracing and barely changes. Results without canopies are identical. Source only; no release packaged.
- **2D auto-size: stiffer column swap.** When the auto-size's local search stops improving, it now swaps each column to the stiffest section of no greater cost and retries the cheaper moves elsewhere, adopting only an exactly verified net saving. Single cheaper moves could be blocked by a coupled cost: on PortalTest1 (36 m three-sided truss, 6 m canopy), reducing only the right column 610UB101 -> 530UB92.4 made the frame flexible enough to need a chord doubler (+$8,091), but with the left column 460UB82.1 -> 530UB82.0 (same 82.4 kg/m, deeper) no doubler is needed. Auto-size now finds that frame: $128,604.61 against $130,695.25 (44 s against 35 s). Factory defaults: the same auto-size and optimiser results; each stuck auto-size spends about 7 s on swap trials (optimiser 56 s to 72 s).
- **Optimise frame + bracing tries full roof bracing and light-rod sizing.** Every bracing layout is now also sized from the lightest priced rods (the fast auto-size did not come far down from the practical layout's heavy sections), keeping the cheaper pass, and the practical layout is re-sized from light rods too. A new option braces the roof in every bay (walls and end-wall restraint as the end-bay layout, with intermediate wall bays for the braced-bay spacing rule); the workspace's Find layout sequence is unchanged. Bracingtest1 (30 x 40 m three-sided shed, 6 m canopy): full roof bracing with 16 mm wire roof braces, $90,826.86 total against $93,633.56 (two-strip eave girder with 24 mm rods). Factory defaults: same $38,514.03 design.
- **3D global sway is measured on the main building, not canopy edges.** The H/n longitudinal sway check (and the SLS stiffness screen) now excludes open canopy edge nodes; canopy braces are still strength-checked. On Bracingtest1 the canopy edge had governed at 0.995 with the main building at 0.77.
- **30 mm rod is priced.** The M28 forged turnbuckle for 30 mm rod uses a user-adopted $40 (the Costing table has no M28), so 30 mm rod is now a sizing option instead of failing with "Enter a fitting price for M28". A blank saved price uses the default.
- **Optimise frame + bracing: safer frame bound.** The search skips bracing options using the frame sized for the stiffest bracing as the least frame cost. The 2D auto-size can break that (canopy test project: $55,115 with the most restraint against $54,807 unrestrained), which could skip a cheaper option. Now, when that frame costs more than the unrestrained frame, a frame for the least restraint is sized as a probe. If it is no cheaper either, the search stops (restraint does not help); otherwise its cost is the bound and each remaining option's own frame is sized. Projects where restraint reduces the frame are unchanged. Canopy test project: same result, 139 s (one extra 2D auto-size).
- **3D bracing: open canopies (phase 5: documentation).** `BRACING_ANALYSIS.md` covers the canopy and open-end scope, guards, friction surfaces, free-roof coefficients, canopy loads and network, the canopy edge purlin, the known difference from 2D on leeward pitched canopies, and canopy results in the optimiser. `BRACING_LAYOUT.md` covers canopy faces, schema v2, carry-over and the canopy bracing rule. `BRACING_DESIGN.md` covers the `canopy_brace` group and canopy edge purlin struts. `CODEMAP.md` lists the canopy functions and `bracing_canopy_regression.py`.
- **3D bracing: open canopies (phase 4: layout editor, layout search and optimiser).** **Optimise frame + bracing** and **Find layout and size** now work on buildings with open canopies. Every searched layout braces each canopy in the same bays as the main roof bracing (the complete roof-braced bays, else any roof-braced bay; a blocked canopy panel moves to the nearest allowed bay; a canopy side already braced is kept), and the stored layout includes them. The 3D location editor draws the canopy roof faces with labels and reference lines (pin/block them like other panels; face filter "Left/Right canopy roof"), shows analysed canopy members, and keeps the main building's saved panels when only a canopy is added or changed. A fixed layout that leaves a canopy unbraced gets a specific message instead of a generic instability. Test project with 5 m and 6 m canopies: the optimiser adopts the unrestrained frame with the continuous left-eave roof girder layout and canopy bracing in the two end bays (10 mm wire rope), $63,814.69 total, in 104 s; the adopted frame and workspace checks match. Results without canopies are identical. Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: open canopies (phase 3: sizing, checks and cost).** Canopy roof X-braces are their own `canopy_brace` design group: a "Canopy roof X braces" selector in the 3D workspace (only for buildings with a canopy, starting at the roof brace size), member checks, material/fitting cost, the per-panel installation allowance, auto-sizing (including SLS stiffness proposals and cost reduction), the project brace-family preference (the unclad-wall rod rule does not apply to a canopy roof), saved workspace settings and diagnostics ("canopy roof" peak tension, "left/right canopy edge purlin" strut locations). The canopy edge purlin lines are checked and sized as struts like the roof lines. Test project with 5 m and 6 m canopies and canopy braces in the end bays: the fast 3D sizing passes at $9,085.19 with 10 mm wire-rope canopy braces. Buildings without a canopy have no canopy group, and their results are identical. Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: open canopies (phase 2: geometry and network).** Buildings with an open canopy (not a lean-to) are no longer rejected by the 3D analysis. The bracing layout geometry records each canopy (plan width and fall, schema `bracing-layout.v2` only when a canopy exists) and adds a braceable canopy roof face, one strip per bay from the main eave line to the canopy edge. Each portal is condensed onto the roof stations plus the canopy edges; the canopy edge purlin is a strut line after the roof stations, and canopy X-braces are their own `canopy_brace` kind. Canopy friction (top and underside) is shared between the eave line and canopy edge, and is included in the force balance and load-path checks. New canopy edge purlin inputs (Sections > Canopies: section, Auto = lightest passing, and spacing, blank = main roof spacing) with a Metroll check using canopy G/Q and the open-underside wind; it feeds the canopy-edge strut checks. Saved bracing locations carry over when only a canopy changes. Guards: lean-tos, skillion roofs with canopies and canopies with an eave-height override are rejected with an explanation. 3D canopy side-wind loads match the 2D frame within 0.6%, except the leeward canopy in pitched-gable "−" cases, where the 3D model keeps the downwind suction (as it does for the main roof) instead of 2D's zero alternative. Canopy braces are not yet sized, checked or costed, and the layout editor and optimiser do not place canopy braces yet (phases 3 and 4). Results for buildings without canopies are identical. Source only; no release packaged.
- **3D bracing: open-ended buildings and roof friction (canopy support, phase 1).** A building with both end walls open is now analysed instead of rejected: its along-building load is roof friction, applied to the top and underside of the main roof (only when both end walls are open; one open end keeps top-only friction). A fully open structure (every wall open) uses the 2D frame's free-roof net coefficients in 3D (B.4(A) for end wind, B.5 windward/leeward for side wind, no internal pressure). Groundwork for canopies, not yet enabled (canopies are still rejected until their 3D roof face exists): canopy G/Q/solar and wind loads are computed per frame in 3D (end wind: the main roof's per-frame bands on top and the open underside's Table 5.1(B) single-opening Cpi; side wind: continuous roof bands measured from the windward canopy tip, as 2D), and canopy friction (top and underside) is recorded per side. The canopy Cpi rule is shared with the 2D frame through `wind.open_canopy_cpi` (2D results unchanged). Results for buildings with a clad end wall and no canopy are identical. New `bracing_canopy_regression.py`. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Optimise frame + bracing** is about 5.6x faster with identical results: factory defaults 340 s to 61 s, same $38,514.03 design and the same 3D bracing frontier. The independent 3D option checks and girder-layout sizings run in worker processes (`bracing_parallel.py`; `PORTALCALC_BRACING_WORKERS=1` runs them in-process). The frame sized for the stiffest bracing is checked with each cheaper option first; if it verifies with that option's own bracing it is the least-cost frame and no 2D re-size is needed. Search-only 3D analyses skip the report-only portal member end forces, and plotting is suspended during the run. Faster 2D solves throughout: no deep copies in the web demand envelope, vectorised 3D force/moment balance, cheaper flybrace cache keys, and load combinations post-processed without a thread pool (the GIL made it slower: 1.08 s threaded v 0.96 s serial per solve). Workers need the calling script's code under `if __name__ == '__main__':` (the app's `main.py` calls `multiprocessing.freeze_support()` for the frozen build); a script without it now gets a clear error from its workers and falls back to running the checks itself. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Optimise frame + bracing** 2D auto-sizes are faster with identical results (a 24 m truss test project 112 s to about 75 s; factory defaults 61 s to 53 s): each auto-size now uses the same per-run load-vector cache as Optimise Structure, frame element stiffness/transformation matrices are cached until geometry or section changes, element force summaries compute end forces once instead of once per station, and undo history is suspended during the run (one snapshot of the adopted design).
- Fixed **Optimise frame + bracing** adopting a different 2D frame from the one it reported when the unrestrained frame won and its auto-size had chosen a non-default truss panel count. The optimiser's state capture/restore did not keep the panel count, so the adopted frame was rebuilt at the default count for its depth (test project: 24 panels, $52,190, instead of the sized 32 panels, $51,334, understating the reported total by $856; it passed only because the final re-solve re-picked the webs). Verified pairs and the adopted design are now restored exactly (sections, panel count and per-member web assignments), the panel count is held between solves during the run (as Optimise Structure does), the adopted count is kept for later solves until an input changes, and Cancel restores the previous panel count too. The adopted 2D frame is re-checked and the report says whether it is the frame that was verified. Restrained sizings still start from the unrestrained sections at the default panel count (the auto-size re-chooses it); starting from the exact unrestrained frame gave a dearer test-project result ($58,092 v $57,126). Results are otherwise unchanged on factory defaults and the test project. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Optimise frame + bracing** now shows a percentage progress bar: "Step n of 4" with the current detail (2D auto-size stage and exact solves, 3D layouts and options checked "k of n"), percent and a ticking elapsed time. Phase shares come from a factory-default run, and the bar only moves forward. Cancel waits for the current 2D auto-size to finish (it cannot be interrupted safely), shows "Cancelling...", keeps the window responsive and restores the previous design. `MainWindow.optimisation_progress_listener` lets a caller follow the 2D auto-size's 12 steps.
- `smoke_checks.py` runs its steps in parallel processes (`--jobs`, default 6; `--jobs 1` for the old serial run) with one BLAS thread per process: about 13 min to 90-100 s. Multi-threaded BLAS was slowing the small dense solves (one suite 28 s single-threaded v 46 s).

The 3D model then ignored the saved blocked panels, so the search could brace a blocked panel. The optimiser now stops before changing anything and explains how to update the saved locations. - **Optimise frame + bracing** now finishes by running the 3D workspace's own Analyse and check on the adopted bracing and saving that result, so the workspace reopens on the checked design (members clickable, governing utilisation) instead of an unanalysed layout where a click toggled a panel and cleared it. - **Changes 3D bracing results.** Extended the adopted rod slack-play acceptance to neutral zero-force rods (for example X-panel diagonals under pure gravity) and to several zero-stiffness modes at once. A case is accepted when every movement, including any combination of modes, extends a zero-force or slack rod within the play limit; combinations are checked exactly. Neutral rods previously always left stability unverified, which blocked lighter frame/bracing pairs in the frame + bracing optimiser. Setting the play limit to 0 restores the former behaviour. Source only; no release packaged.
- Added **Bracing > Optimise frame + bracing together...**: sizes the 2D frame and 3D bracing together for the lowest total estimated cost. Builds a frontier of bracing options and the frame restraint each gives, sizes the frame at the stiffest level to bound the search, sizes frames only for options that could win, and verifies the winner exactly with the restraint adopted. Uses the workspace's saved bracing setup; Cancel restores the previous design. Factory defaults: 219 s, end-bay bracing with the unrestrained frame wins. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Find layout and size** (practical) now re-sizes a passing input layout instead of only verifying it, compares open-side end-wall restraint for sheds braced on one side wall (any span/eave), and tries an extra wall bay alone before adding its roof collector. Each alternative is adopted only if exactly checked and cheaper or preferred. The supplied three-sided 24 x 40 m shed now finds $7,654 (open-side end-wall bracing, 20 mm rod walls) instead of keeping $9,964 with EA 75x75x6 walls. Source only; no release packaged.
- **Changes 3D bracing results.** Adopted acceptance of bounded rod slack play: a case with one zero-stiffness mode that takes up a slack rod either way within **Accepted rod slack play** (Sections / criteria, default 1.0 mm, 0 for none) now counts as stable. Several modes, larger play and neutral zero-force rods remain unverified. Existing presets take the default, so lighter bracing can now pass: the supplied three-sided 24 x 40 m shed re-sizes from $9,964 (24 mm rod roof, EA 75x75x6 walls) to $8,294 on its current layout, and to $7,944 with open-side end-wall bracing (20 mm rod walls). Source only; no release packaged.
- Fixed 3D bracing analyses aborting with "Unstable or ill-conditioned model" when rods are slack rather than a mechanism, e.g. both diagonals of a roof X panel slightly shortened after end-wall bracing is added. The case now completes and reports the play before a slack rod engages and which rods take it up; a true mechanism still raises. Stability stays unverified, so such designs still do not pass; sizing keeps the best verified design and retries with the last stable braces, as before. A three-sided 24 x 40 m shed showed 0.21 mm play with open-side end-wall braces. Source only; no release packaged.
- Added **End-wall portal restraint > 3D bracing model**: the 2D portal adopts the least interior-frame sway restraint found by the 3D bracing analysis, applied as one spring on the mean eave movement (frame sway only; eave spread is not resisted). Adopted on 3D workspace Save and close from a passing, taut-stable result and saved with the preset. Bracing layout or section changes make it stale (no credit); portal section, load or wind changes keep it with a recheck warning. While active, 3D sizing keeps brace stiffness at or above the credited bracing. End-bay-only roof bracing gives no credit because middle frames receive no restraint.
